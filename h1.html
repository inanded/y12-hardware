Show me step by step

Ask two questions about each item.

  1. Is it only needed while the speaker is on, and fine to lose when the power goes? Then it is in RAM (fast, can be changed, lost when the power goes).
  2. Is it the instructions the speaker needs to start up? Then it is in ROM (can only be read, kept with no power).

Inside a magnetic hard disk

About 6 minutes · watch, then check
The story

A failing hard disk often makes a repeated clicking sound. Technicians call it the click of death. The read/write head flies a few nanometres above a platter spinning thousands of times a minute. If the head touches the platter (a head crash), it scrapes off the magnetic coating and the data there is gone.

Why it matters for exams: hard disks have moving parts, so they can be damaged by knocks. An SSD has no moving parts. That is one reason laptops and phones use solid-state storage.

Check: put the hard disk steps in order

Show me step by step

Think of it from the outside in.

  1. What is it made of? Platters that can be magnetised, inside a sealed box.
  2. How does it move? On a spindle, spinning fast.
  3. What reads it? A head on an arm, moved by electronic circuits.
  4. How is the surface organised? Tracks and sectors; one track in one sector is a block.
  5. How are the bits stored and read? As a magnetic pattern; reading turns a change in field into a change in current.

Inside an SSD (flash memory)

About 4 minutes · watch, then check

Check: fill the gaps

Show me step by step

Picture the floating gate as a sealed box. Writing pushes electrons into the box. Because the box is sealed by insulation, the electrons cannot get out, even with the power off. Reading asks one question: does current flow through the cell or not? That answer is the bit.

Go further

Beyond ASWhy do SSDs wear out? Each flash cell can only be erased and rewritten a limited number of times, because pushing electrons through the insulating layer slowly damages it. The SSD controller spreads writes evenly across all the cells (wear levelling) so no cell wears out long before the others. Find out what TBW means on an SSD specification.

Inside an optical disc reader/writer

About 4 minutes · watch, then check

Check: put the optical steps in order

Show me step by step

A pit is a tiny dip in the surface. A land is the flat part between pits. A laser bounces off them differently, a bit like light from a torch bouncing off a flat mirror compared with a dented one. The sensor notices the difference, and the differences become 1s and 0s.

Solid state (flash) memory: model answer

  • An SSD has no moving parts.
  • Data is stored in flash memory using NAND or NOR technology.
  • Each cell has 1 or 2 transistors.
  • The floating gate traps (retains) electrons, so the data is kept without power.
  • The control gate allows or stops current passing through the cell.
  • To read, the controller checks whether current flows, giving a 1 or a 0.

Optical disc reader/writer: model answer

  • The disc is spun at high speed.
  • A laser is shone onto the disc; the optical head moves it into position.
  • It follows a spiral track from the centre outwards.
  • Writing: the laser burns pits to represent the data.
  • Reading: the laser reflects from pits and lands; the reflection from a pit is different from a land.
  • A sensor detects the reflected light and the differences are interpreted as 1s and 0s.

How these questions are marked

Each question asks for two reasons. Each reason gets 1 mark, and a second mark for linking it to the system in the question.

Not enough: "Hard disks are cheaper."
Full marks: "Magnetic storage costs less per unit of storage, and the system stores large video files, so it needs a very large capacity."

Show me step by step

Two reasons that work for systems that record or store lots of video:

  • Cost: magnetic storage costs less per gigabyte … so very large capacity is affordable for big video files.
  • Lasting longer: the system writes all the time (a very large number of read/write operations) … and magnetic storage lasts longer under that much writing.

Sentence starter: "Magnetic storage is more suitable because … This matters for this system because …"
